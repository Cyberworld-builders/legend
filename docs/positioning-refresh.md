# CyberWorld positioning refresh

**Working date:** 2026-10-13  
**Status:** First-pass direction and landing-page prototype

## North star

CyberWorld Builders is Jay's independent technology workshop: a public record of the systems, creative tools, and unusual experiences he chooses to explore. The site should make curiosity and craft visible. It should not behave like a software agency funnel or imply that every interest needs to become a service.

## Current context

- Jay works full-time with Platinum.
- He keeps a small number of long-running client collaborations.
- CyberWorld is the home for personal and exploratory work that does not fit those roles.
- The current site still leads with services, sales proof, contact capture, FAQ, local-service metadata, and search-oriented copy.
- Recent writing points toward agent tooling and human–AI work, media and music technology, and immersive or horror experiences.

## Editorial pillars

1. **Agent systems and engineering practice** — harnesses, memory, context, tools, infrastructure, and the practical work around AI models.
2. **Creative media tools** — listening, annotation, music technology, human performance, AI-assisted remixing, and eventually video.
3. **Immersive and physical experiences** — horror, mixed reality, interactive puzzles, location-based experiences, and technology that deepens a feeling.

Infrastructure, Linux, security, and software craft can appear as connective tissue when they support one of those pursuits.

## Landing-page shape

1. **Identity:** “Useful systems. Unusual ideas.” Establish CyberWorld as a workshop, not a provider menu.
2. **Current coordinates:** Be candid about the full-time Platinum role, selected long-running client work, and the personal purpose of CyberWorld.
3. **Workbench:** Show the three editorial pillars as active questions, each leading into a representative field note.
4. **Field notes:** Curate current writing that reflects the direction. Do not let a popularity scorer decide the public identity.
5. **About:** A short human explanation of Jay and why the workshop exists.
6. **Contact:** Keep a quiet email link for people already in Jay's orbit or a thoughtful invitation. No inquiry CTA, review strip, lead form, chatbot, or conversion language.

## Navigation and content boundaries

- Primary navigation: Workbench, Field Notes, About.
- Keep the logo, terminal-derived visual language, dark palette, and green accent. Use the green as an accent and let writing, project artifacts, and imagery carry the page.
- Remove service cards, client reviews, Upwork links, sales FAQs, and default service/lead-generation structured data from the primary experience.
- Existing service routes are left intact in this first pass to avoid breaking old links. Decide separately whether to archive, redirect, or retire them after reviewing inbound links and any remaining client use.
- Keep the full blog archive available for now. Curate the homepage around current interests, then classify old business and marketing pieces as archive material before considering redirects or deletion.
- Do not invent internal details about Platinum or imply it is a CyberWorld product. The public mention should stay at the level Jay supplied: a full-time role with Platinum.

## How to judge the next iteration

- A friend should understand in a few seconds what Jay cares about now.
- The page should feel like a place to follow work and ideas, not a pitch deck.
- Every featured item should connect to a real project, prototype, or piece of writing.
- There should be no pressure to contact, buy, book, or qualify as a lead.
- The design should stay recognizable as CyberWorld without borrowing the structure of a freelancer or agency template.

## Prototype scope

The first pass reshapes the homepage, navigation, footer, default metadata, author identity, and global structured data. It uses existing logos, the current background texture, and existing post art. It does not delete or redirect service pages, rewrite old posts, or change the publishing pipeline.
